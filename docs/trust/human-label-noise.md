# Human label noise: the term the guarantee was missing

**Date:** 2026-08-21
**Status:** machinery shipped and enforced; the human measurement is pre-registered and NOT yet run.

Per-table certification rests on two assumptions. One was enforced from the start, the other was
never even stated as a quantity.

* **Exchangeability** holds by construction, because the calibration data *is* the table. That is
  the whole reason local certification is reachable where global certification is not.
* **The labels are right.** This was assumed. It is false whenever the labeller is a human.

## The arithmetic

Certification measures `p_tilde`, the error rate *as judged by whoever labelled*. Write `beta` for
the labeller's false-accept rate (they call a wrong repair correct) and `gamma` for the
false-reject rate. Then

```
p_tilde = p (1 - beta) + (1 - p) gamma
p       = (p_tilde - gamma) / (1 - beta - gamma)
```

**`gamma = 0` is the conservative choice, and that is derived rather than assumed.** `dp/dgamma`
has the sign of `p_tilde - 1 + beta`, negative whenever `beta < 1 - p_tilde` -- true throughout
the regime of interest, where `p_tilde` is a few percent. So `p` *decreases* in `gamma`, and
`gamma = 0` gives the largest admissible `p`:

```
p <= p_tilde / (1 - beta)
```

That is a factor, not a rounding error:

| `beta` | true error rate when a measured 0.05 is certified |
| --- | --- |
| 0.00 | 0.0500 |
| 0.10 | 0.0556 |
| 0.20 | 0.0625 |
| 0.50 | **0.1000** |

## Why the noise is in the dangerous direction, not the safe one

Automation bias. The labeller is shown the machine's proposed value and asked "is this correct?".
That is an acquiescence-biased ratification task, so wrongly accepting is systematically likelier
than wrongly rejecting: `beta >> gamma` **by construction of the interface**, not by accident of
the population.

This matters because the label-noise literature's reassurance is conditional and the condition
fails here. Einbinder, Feldman, Bates, Angelopoulos, Gendler and Romano
([arXiv:2209.14295](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14295), JMLR) show conformal and risk-controlling
methods "attain conservative risk over the clean ground truth labels **whenever the noise is
dispersive and increases variability**", and are vulnerable to noise that *reduces* apparent
uncertainty. Their positive results for general losses (Propositions 4 and 5) cover the
false-negative rate in **multi-label classification and segmentation** under a vector-flip model
with `P(flip) < 0.5`. They do not cover a scalar accept/reject loss on a machine-proposed value,
and acquiescence noise is precisely the variability-*reducing* kind.

So the honest reading is not "the guarantee degrades gracefully". It is: **the guarantee survives
only under a condition this application systematically violates, so `beta` must be measured.**

## How `beta` is bounded: planted controls

Nothing in the labelling stream reveals `beta`, because every real item's truth is exactly what is
being asked. A **planted control** breaks that circularity: an item whose correct value is known
*before* the labeller sees it. Every `correct` verdict on one is an observed false accept.

The reported bound splits `delta` evenly across the two estimates so their union still holds at
`1 - delta`:

```
p <= CP_upper(errors, n, delta/2) / (1 - CP_upper(false_accepts, controls, delta/2))
```

Implemented as `dataforge.conformal.label_noise_adjusted_bound`.

### The sample budget, which is the point

Roughly double the naive floor, and still an afternoon of a data steward's attention. This is why
the finding is a price rather than a refutation.

| alpha | naive floor | with controls | total human judgements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.05 | 59 | n=82, k=30 | **112** (1.9x) |
| 0.10 | 29 | n=40, k=30 | **70** (2.4x) |
| 0.20 | 14 | n=19, k=30 | **49** (3.5x) |

## The category error this design could have made

**Planted controls estimate `beta` on the plant distribution, not on the corrector's real error
distribution.** If plants are easier to reject than genuine corrector mistakes, `beta` is
understated, the "noise-corrected" bound stays anti-conservative, and the result is a
rigorous-looking guarantee that is not one. It would be *worse* than the unadjusted bound, because
it carries the authority of having been checked.

Three mitigations, all mandatory rather than advisory:

1. **Plants are drawn from the column's own empirical values** (`origin="column_distribution"`),
   so a plant is as plausible as a real mistake. An obviously-wrong plant measures the labeller's
   attention, not their false-accept rate on hard cases.
2. **A second control class is generated by the corrector itself** on cells whose truth is known
   (`origin="corrector_generated"`), so at least one class is distributionally identical to real
   corrector errors.
3. **The certificate records the scope**, in `SessionCertification.beta_scope_note`, so the
   caveat travels with the artifact instead of relying on a reader's memory. `beta` must never be
   quoted as "the labeller's error rate".

## What is enforced in code

* `CalibrationSessionArtifact.label_source` is **required with no default**. A default would have
  to be one of two wrong things: `human` breaks every oracle-labelled fixture, and `oracle` lets a
  real user's session be certified as though their judgement were ground truth -- the unsafe
  direction. Forcing every construction site to state it is the fail-closed option.
* `certify_from_session` **raises** when `label_source == "human"` and no planted control carries a
  verdict. A pending plant measures nothing and does not unlock certification.
* Plants live in `planted_controls`, never in `samples`, so a known-wrong item can never be
  certified *on*.
* When the noise term is what refused, the reason string says so explicitly
  (`label_noise_blocked: ...`) rather than blaming the corrector's precision.

## What is NOT yet known

**`beta` has never been measured on a human.** Every certification in this project so far used
RAHA ground truth standing in for a person, which has `beta = 0` by construction and therefore
cannot show any of this. The experiment is pre-registered in
[`eval/preregistration/human_label_noise.md`](../../eval/preregistration/human_label_noise.md)
with its falsifier and kill criterion fixed in advance.

Two things must not be substituted for it:

* **RAHA ground truth.** Using an oracle to estimate a human's false-accept rate would return
  `beta = 0` and manufacture the exact guarantee under examination.
* **An LLM asked to ratify its own proposal.** That measures a model's acquiescence, which is a
  genuinely interesting quantity and a legitimate cheap probe, but it is **not** `beta` and must
  never enter a certificate.

### What the model probe DID establish (2026-08-21)

Run, and worth the $0.15 it cost:

| item class | ratified known-wrong | n | rate | upper bound |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `column_distribution` | 2 | 30 | 0.0667 | 0.2207 |
| `corrector_generated` | 4 | 8 | **0.5000** | 0.8430 |

**A 7.5x gap between the two control classes.** At n=8 the magnitude is unestablished, but the
direction converts mitigation (2) above from an argued requirement into an evidenced one: real
corrector errors are ratified far more readily than column-resampled plants, so **the classes
cannot be pooled**, and a plant-only `beta` would understate the false-accept rate substantially.
This is the strongest available reason to keep both classes mandatory.

It says nothing about a human. A model has no automation bias toward a peer's suggestion in the way
a person has toward a machine's, so if anything a human's `beta` on `corrector_generated` items
should be expected to be *higher*, not lower — which is the direction that breaks the guarantee.

## The moat, stated so it can be falsified

The obvious objection to this whole project: if a frontier model can clean a table competently, why
does DataForge exist? The answer is **not** that models cannot judge their own work. That claim is
false and the repo already refutes it — the structured-enum corrector's self-confidence discriminates
well (ROC-AUC **0.862** at k=3, **0.948** at k=9, against **0.5536** [0.500, 0.617] for free text,
in `eval/results/corrector_arm_sweep.json`).

The claim is narrower and testable:

> **An ordering is not a guarantee.** A model can rank its proposals well. It cannot state that at
> a given threshold at most `alpha` of its writes to *this* table are wrong, with `1 - delta`
> confidence — because that requires labelled calibration data drawn from the table itself, plus a
> bound on the labeller's own error rate. AUC is a ranking statistic and implies nothing about the
> error rate at any threshold.

**How to falsify it.** Show a model producing a *calibrated* absolute error bound on an unseen table
that holds up against ground truth — i.e. it says "at threshold t, my error rate here is at most
0.05" and repeated trials on fresh tables put the realised rate inside that bound at the claimed
frequency. If that works, the calibration layer is redundant and this project's core asset is gone.

Two pieces of evidence currently point the other way, and both are committed rather than argued:
`type_mismatch` scores **1.0000** on hospital and **0.0000** on other datasets under the same model;
and triage quality depends on both dataset and model (rayyan 0.955 on `gpt-5.6-sol`, 0.656 on
`gpt-5-mini`) with **neither dependence knowable at runtime without labels**. A model that cannot
tell which regime it is in cannot bound its own error in that regime.
